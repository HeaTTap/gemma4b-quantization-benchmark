Qualitative Analysis: Decoupling the "Intelligence Gap"

Evaluating the raw response logs shows that quantization errors manifest primarily in multi-step constraint evaluation and spatial dimension projection. Below is the detailed breakdown:

1) 3D Spatial Rotation and Projection Defect (Q7)

In the 3D die rotation problem, the uncompressed BF16 model successfully tracked all three rotations (S0 → S1 → S2 → S3) to correctly deduce that the number on the front face would be 5 (Option C). The Q4 model, however, lost orientation during the spatial transformations, concluding that the front face was 1 (Option A). This indicates that the geometric/spatial semantic relationships embedded in the model's high-dimensional weights are highly sensitive to the precision loss of 4-bit quantization.

2) Mathematical Hallucination & Option-Forced Fitting (Q4)

In the recursive bitwise function mystery(13, 11), the BF16 model performed the bitwise operations correctly, identifying that 16 & 8 = 0, which naturally terminated the recursion at 24 (Option C) with the explanation that the function calculates binary addition (13 + 11 = 24). In contrast, the Q4 model suffered from mathematical hallucinations during the dry-run, claiming that 16 & 8 = 8 and 24 & 16 = 16. This incorrect arithmetic led the model to evaluate the recursion to 40. Realizing that 40 was not among the multiple-choice options, the Q4 model performed an arbitrary post-hoc rationalization, claiming the recursion should halt at the intermediate state of 24 to match Option C. This demonstrates that while quantized models can sometimes select the correct final option due to multiple-choice constraint boundaries, their underlying reasoning is prone to computational noise.

3) High-Order Epistemic Reasoning Limits (Q5)

Both models failed the Cheryl's Birthday logic puzzle. The BF16 model identified the logical constraints but declared the problem "ambiguous" and selected a placeholder (May 16), while the Q4 model incorrectly executed the elimination steps. This suggests that complex multi-agent reasoning is a boundary limit for the base 4B architecture size, regardless of quantization.
